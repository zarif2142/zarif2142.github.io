P&G, Software Engineering Intern

Access reviews that went from 30 minutes to under 2 per user, a 1,300-user migration inside a perfect SOX audit, and a vision prototype at 98% accuracy.

Access reviews and clean-up

The daily work was access reviews and leaver clean-up: checking who still had access they should not have, and removing it. Done by hand, each user took 30 minutes. I automated it with Python, Java, Selenium and GitHub Actions, taking each user from 30 minutes to under 2. The point was not just speed. A manual review drifts. An automated one asks the same questions every time, and leaves a record of the answers.

That record mattered more than I expected. When someone asks why an account was removed, the answer should be a log, not a memory.

The migration and the audit

The bigger job was a bulk provisioning tool for a large platform migration: 1,300+ users, moved at 100% accuracy, as part of a perfect 8/8 SOX audit. Accuracy was the whole requirement. One wrong account in a migration is a security problem and an audit finding at the same time. The tool checked every user before it moved them, and the audit passed 8 out of 8.

I learned that boring code is the point in this kind of work. The clever part was not in the tool. It was in refusing to move a user the tool could not verify.

The Gemini prototype

The most different piece was a Gemini vision prototype that turns smart-glasses video into structured data at 98% accuracy. It was a prototype, not a product, and the job was to find out whether the idea worked at all. It did, well enough to show to the team and talk about what a real version would need. Prototypes earn their keep by answering one question clearly, and this one did.
