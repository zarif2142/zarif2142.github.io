OpenRing

OpenRing is an iPhone app for the COLMI R06, a smart ring that costs about $20 on AliExpress. It reads health data straight off the ring over Bluetooth and keeps everything on my iPhone. No sketchy app, no cloud, and ownership of my data.

Why

The ring is good hardware for the price, but the official app wants an account and sends your health data to the company. I wanted the data without the strings, and the ability to customize an iPhone app for my own goals.

What I built

  • RingKit, a standalone Swift package that speaks the ring's undocumented Bluetooth Low Energy protocol: checksummed binary packets, BCD timestamps and multi-packet transfers. It's covered by 108 XCTest cases at 93% line coverage.
  • A SwiftUI app with an async/await CoreBluetooth layer that reconnects on its own and syncs in the background. It stores 7 health metrics in SwiftData (steps, heart rate, SpO2 and stress among them) and charts them by day, week and month with Swift Charts.
  • A home-screen widget, health alerts, resting heart rate and export to Apple Health.

What was hard

Code that looked right on paper kept breaking the moment real bytes came off the ring, so I started testing against the actual device early and often. The open-source Gadgetbridge project was my best reference for the protocol. Live heart rate and SpO2 only lock on about half the time, and I proved that's a limit of the ring itself by reproducing it with a reference client.

Where it's at

It works on my ring every day.
